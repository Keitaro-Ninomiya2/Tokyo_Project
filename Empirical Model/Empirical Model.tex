\subsection{Drafting specification}

We estimate the causal effect of military drafting on office-level outcomes using the specifications below. The baseline unit of observation is position $\times$ kakari $\times$ year (1938--1945), unless noted. All specifications include year fixed effects and either ka (section) or kyoku (department) fixed effects, plus position fixed effects. Standard errors are clustered at the office level.

Incoming transfers (Tables~\ref{tab:reallocation} and~\ref{tab:transfer-decomposition}). Count outcomes (transfers in) are modeled with OLS:
\begin{equation}
Y_{j,p,t} = \beta_1 D_{j,p,t} + \beta_2 \log(M_{j,p,t}+1) + \gamma + \delta_p + \lambda_t + \varepsilon_{j,p,t},
\end{equation}
where $Y_{j,p,t}$ is the number of transfers into position $p$ in office $j$ in year $t$; $D_{j,p,t}$ is the number of male workers drafted from that position; and $M_{j,p,t}$ is the cumulative male stock. The coefficient $\beta_1$ measures the change in the expected number of transfers associated with one additional draft from the same position.

New hiring (Table~\ref{tab:reallocation-newhires}). We use the same count specification with the number of new hires as $Y_{j,p,t}$.

Short-run promotion (Table~\ref{tab:promotion}). Using a linear probability model, we regress a promotion indicator (rank increased from $t-1$ to $t$) on the number of drafts from the same office, for non-drafted workers observed in consecutive years. Fixed effects are year, ka, and position. We interact the treatment with gender, rank, and engineer indicators.

Bonus salary (Table~\ref{tab:rinji}). We regress the number of workers receiving \emph{rinji tokubetsu} (temporary special) allowances on the number of drafts, in the 1944 cross-section. The unit of observation is position $\times$ office. Fixed effects are ka and position.

Retention (Table~\ref{tab:retention}). We regress an indicator for whether a non-drafted worker appears in the next year's directory on the number of drafts from the same office. Fixed effects are year, ka, and position.

Donor offices (Table~\ref{tab:donor}). We compare donor office--years (those with at least one outbound transfer) to non-donors at the ka $\times$ position $\times$ year level, with kyoku, position, and year fixed effects and log headcount where noted. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:donor_lagged} tests whether hiring differences predate transfers; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:transferred_chars} summarizes selection into transfer within donors.

Long-run outcomes of transferred workers (Table~\ref{tab:transfer-outcomes}). We compare movers to destination and origin peers with kakari-, year-, and position-level fixed effects as in the table. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:donor_postwar} reports postwar rank and tenure for workers who remained in donor offices.

Gender composition and tenure (Table~\ref{tab:gender}). Female share of workers and average tenure at the position $\times$ kakari $\times$ year level are modeled by OLS. The sample excludes \emph{kanri} (higher-tier civil servant) positions where noted. Treatment is the count of drafted males from the same office--position. We control for cumulative male baseline and the same fixed effects as above.

Wartime draft shock and postwar female integration (Table~\ref{tab:reduced-form-female}). We regress postwar female outcomes in a \emph{kakaricho}'s office on the wartime draft shock (share of males drafted) in that manager's wartime office, at the kakari $\times$ year level after 1947. Fixed effects condition on the kakaricho's wartime \emph{ka} and wartime position; some specifications add postwar year fixed effects.

Postwar tenure (Table~\ref{tab:future}). For non-drafted workers observed in 1944, we regress postwar tenure (count of years appearing in civil service through 1955) on drafting exposure in their 1944 office. The unit of observation is the individual; treatment is any drafted or draft share; fixed effects are ka (or kyoku) and position as of 1944.

\subsection{Identification strategy}
\label{sec:identification}

Our estimand is the causal effect of military drafting on personnel outcomes, holding fixed the fine organizational and occupational structure in which a worker or office is observed. The identifying assumption is \emph{conditional exogeneity}: conditional on the fixed effects included in each specification---typically year, section (\emph{ka}), and position, and sometimes bureau (\emph{kyoku})---variation in the number of workers drafted from a position is orthogonal to unobserved shocks in the structural error term. Equivalently, potential outcomes for hiring, transfers, promotion, and retention do not differ systematically across draft counts once those fixed effects and baseline male counts are held constant.

This assumption would fail if personnel offices systematically assigned conscription risk or drafted workers on the basis of unobserved productivity shocks that also drive our outcomes. We mitigate concern about unobserved heterogeneity across large, coarse groups by including \emph{ka} (section) fixed effects: Figure~\ref{fig:tokyo-hierarchy} illustrates how \emph{ka} sits in the Tokyo-To hierarchy as the section at which substantive portfolios are organized, so comparisons are primarily within the same policy unit and job title. Year fixed effects absorb aggregate shocks to the draft and the wartime bureaucracy; position fixed effects absorb differences across standardized job titles (clerical, engineering, and so on). The draft count itself is determined outside the office by nationwide conscription eligibility (age, health, student or family exemptions), which supports treating residual variation as conditionally exogenous to local human-resource strategy once organizational cell and occupation are controlled for, and the pre-war balance evidence in Table~\ref{tab:balance} is consistent with this identifying assumption.

\subsection{Balance of pre-war outcomes conditional on \emph{ka} and occupation}

Table~\ref{tab:balance} evaluates whether offices that later experienced drafting looked different \emph{before} the war, using the pre-war panel (1934--1937, strictly before any military drafting began in 1938) restricted to units also observed in 1944. The unit of observation is \emph{kakari} $\times$ position $\times$ year, as in the main regressions. Panel~A compares unconditional means between cells with and without any drafting in 1944: drafted-in-1944 units display a much lower female share and a higher new-hire share, so simple cross-sectional comparisons confound drafting with baseline churn and gender mix.

Panel~B addresses this by regressing each pre-war outcome on the 1944 male draft count (continuous, matching the treatment variable in our main regressions) while including \emph{ka} fixed effects, position fixed effects, and year fixed effects---the same conditioning dimensions used in the main regressions---and controlling for log office headcount. After partialing out section, position, year, and size, the coefficient on future draft intensity is statistically indistinguishable from zero for every reported outcome: female share ($p=0.15$), new-hire share ($p=0.74$), promoted share ($p=0.11$), external transfer share ($p=0.70$), and headcount ($p=0.18$). In sum, within section (\emph{ka}) and position cells, pre-war trajectories look parallel across units that would later differ in draft intensity, supporting the conditional exogeneity assumption used for causal interpretation.
